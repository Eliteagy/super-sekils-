# 06 - Soft Skills, Maintenance, and Mentoring

**Tags:** #soft-skills #mentorship #tech-debt #career-growth

## The Realities of Maintenance
After the initial thrill of building systems, the focus shifted to maintenance and operational readiness.
- **Documentation & Onboarding:** Crucial for helping on-call engineers troubleshoot expected problems (e.g., SQS outages, bad proxy configurations).
- **Code Churn:** Observing where code frequently changes ("churn") is a strong indicator of coupling and technical debt. Identifying these areas early allows for strategic refactoring before the codebase becomes unmaintainable.

## Diplomacy and Conflict
Working in a large organization means dealing with varied personalities and management styles.
- Navigating conflicts was stressful and occasionally impacted performance.
- Growth required self-awareness, psychological understanding, and anticipating conflict to proactively manage working relationships.

## Mentoring vs. Training
- **Training:** The author excelled at breaking down complex mental models and explaining systems to peers.
- **Mentoring:** Found mentoring an intern highly challenging. Balancing when to provide guidance versus letting them struggle to figure things out was difficult. Ultimately, the intern delivered an excellent project and secured a full-time offer, relying on a combination of the author's guidance and cross-team subject matter experts.
